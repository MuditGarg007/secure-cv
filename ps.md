CC6-IC04: Next-Generation Passwordless Authentication 1. Problem definition Passwords are reused, guessed, phished and leaked in breaches. SMS OTPs can be intercepted through SIM swaps or handed over to fraudsters on the phone. Standards such as FIDO2/WebAuthn passkeys exist, but adoption in Indian government, education and smallbusiness services is low. Users with basic phones or no smartphone are often left out entirely. 2. Objective Build a working passwordless authentication system that is resistant to phishing, easy to use and inclusive, together with a secure way to recover an account. 3. Key requirements 1. Passwordless login: use passkeys (FIDO2/WebAuthn), device-bound cryptographic keys, biometrics, hardware tokens or a combination. 2. Phishing resistance: credentials must be bound to the genuine site or app, so a look-alike site cannot reuse them. 3. Account recovery: provide a secure recovery path that does not fall back to a weak password or SMS OTP. 4. Multi-device and shared access: support adding a new device and revoking a lost one. 5. Inclusivity: offer an option for users with basic phones or limited literacy, such as a QR code with a trusted device or a voice prompt. 6. Risk-based checks (optional): ask for extra verification when a login looks unusual (new location, new device). 7. Admin view: show registered devices, login history and revoked credentials. 4. Expected deliverable • A demo web or mobile app with end-to-end passwordless sign-up, login, new-device registration and recovery. • A demonstration that a phishing page cannot capture a usable credential. • A short threat model listing attacks covered and not covered. 5. Success criteria Security strength, phishing resistance, quality of the recovery design, user experience, inclusivity and readiness for real deployment.
